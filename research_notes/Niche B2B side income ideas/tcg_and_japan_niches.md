# TCG Store Software and Japan–US Cross-Border B2B Niches (founder-background niches, weighted LOW by request)

Research date: 2026-11-24. About 17 search/fetch calls. Reddit and forum threads could not be retrieved directly, so owner complaints rest on thin evidence (see Gaps).

## Q1: TCG side: market size, software in use, pricing, complaints, gaps

### Takeaway
Demand in the TCG market is real and growing fast: ICv2 puts US/Canada hobby games at +40% to $3.66B in 2025. But store software is already crowded. TCGplayer (owned by eBay) controls the main marketplace and owns BinderPOS. ShadowPOS, Crystal Commerce and at least 7 Shopify TCG apps compete on POS, pricing and Shopify sync, at prices from $0 to $200/month. There is a gap in niche workflows: grading-submission tracking for shops, Japanese-card pricing, and multi-channel sync that includes eBay. A solo founder could plausibly get a few hundred to a few thousand dollars of MRR there. Reaching $10k MRR quickly looks unlikely.

### Cited Findings
**Market size and demand**
- ICv2 estimates US/Canada hobby games grew 40% to $3.66B in 2025, driven by TCGs while other categories were flat or modest — [ICv2](https://icv2.com/articles/markets/view/62035/big-growth-hobby-games-2025)
- ICv2 put 2024 hobby game sales (US + Canada) at about $2.84B and said store counts were "flat to up" in 2024. It gave no absolute count — [ICv2](https://icv2.com/print/article/59119)
- Target said trading card sales were up nearly 70% and on track to pass $1B in 2025. Walmart's Pokémon card sales were reported up more than 10x year over year — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/23/pokemon-card-trading-cards-boom-target-ebay-sales)
- PSA had its biggest grading year ever in 2025, driven by Pokémon — [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/collectibles/pokemon-powers-2025-psa-s-biggest-grading-year-in-company-history)
- The Pokémon Company produced about 10B cards in FY2025/26, bringing the lifetime total past 85B — [PokeGuardian](https://www.pokeguardian.com/3191786_10-billion-pokemon-cards-sold-in-fiscal-year-2025-2026)
- IBISWorld counts about 61k–70k US "Hobby & Toy Stores" (NAICS 45112). That category is far broader than game stores, and IBISWorld's two pages disagree with each other — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/industry-statistics/number-of-businesses/hobby-toy-stores-united-states/)
- I found no authoritative count of US local game stores (LGS) from ICv2 or GAMA (see Gaps).

**Incumbents and pricing**
- **BinderPOS** (owned by TCGplayer/eBay): Binder $100/month, Binder Pro $150/month, plus 2% on own-website sales and 2.5% on sales through the TCGplayer integration (US only), which comes with a 1% marketplace-fee discount. It requires Shopify. **New seller onboarding and sign-ups are paused; waitlist only** (the page gives no date) — [TCGplayer Point of Sale page](https://seller.tcgplayer.com/point-of-sale). In 2020 it cost $129/month including Shopify — [ICv2 2020](https://icv2.com/print/article/44991)
- **ShadowPOS**: $200/month "everything included," plus 2% marketplace integration fee and 2% on public-website sales. Includes buylist, full event/ticket management, and sync with Card Trader and Mana Pool. **It does not list TCGplayer or eBay sync.** It claims "500,000 sq ft of local game stores" but publishes no store count — [ShadowPOS](https://shadowpos.com/)
- **Crystal Commerce**: no official public pricing. A competitor-commissioned study (CardCloud/Winventory) says $100/month plus 2.5% online commission, and 11.75% total on marketplace sales. The same study estimates all-in monthly cost for a sample store at about $4.9k (BinderPOS Pro) and $5.1k (Crystal), including payment processing. That figure is vendor-biased — [Winventory/CardCloud study](https://winventory.ca/partner/study?id=startup); [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/crystalcommerce/pricing)
- **TCGplayer marketplace fees**: from 2026-02-10 the commission cap rose from $50 to $75, and Level 1–4 seller commission went from 10.25% to 10.75% — [TCGplayer Help](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/37531606328727). The Pro fee is 2.5% on shipped online sales — [TCGplayer Fees](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/201357836-Fees). TCGplayer Direct fees were restructured in 2025 — [TCGplayer blog](https://seller.tcgplayer.com/blog/simpler-and-more-predictable-fees-coming-for-tcgplayer-direct-june-18)
- **Shopify TCG apps** (a crowded field with few reviews):
  - blstr: $100/month, real-time Shopify sync, TCGplayer market pricing, barcodes, 0 reviews — [Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/blstr-1)
  - Synq: from $29/month, about 70 reviews at about 4.0 — [Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/synq-tcg-card-manager)
  - TCG Importer: from $69/month. One review complains prices are stale, with cards sitting at a 0.99 default — [Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/tcgapp)
  - Also: LGS Forge (free plan, follows TCGplayer daily), PowerList TCG (multi-marketplace sync, no reviews), Kori TCG Manager, TCG Cards Market Manager, Hobby TCG Trade-In (buylist) — [LGS Forge](https://apps.shopify.com/lgs-forge), [PowerList](https://apps.shopify.com/powerlist-tcg), [Kori](https://apps.shopify.com/kori-tcg-manager), [Market Manager](https://apps.shopify.com/tcg-market-manager), [Trade-In](https://apps.shopify.com/tcg-trade-in)
  - I found no Shopify app built for TCG that clearly syncs eBay listings. eBay sync appears only in general multichannel tools (search result summary; not exhaustively verified).
- **Consumer side (shows willingness to pay, but these are not B2B buyers)**:
  - Collectr: Pro is $7.99/month or $59.99/year. BetaKit reports more than 4M users and eight-figure ARR, bootstrapped and without paid ads — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/how-collectr-bootstrapped-a-trading-card-hobby-into-an-eight-figure-business/); [App Pricing Lab](https://apppricinglab.com/iap/apple/1603892248)
  - CollX raised $5M seed and a $10M Series A; Pro is $10/month — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/07/collx-raises-10m-to-grow-its-card-collection-marketplace)
- **Grading tracking**:
  - Grade Flow Tracker (Shopify): $14.99/month. Shops assign PSA and other grading submissions to customers and track progress. Launched 2025-09-12 by a card-break shop, with no reviews — [Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/grading-submission-tracker)
  - Grade Sync Pro (iOS) claims bulk submission lists and status tracking — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/grade-sync-pro/id6759976779)
- **Japanese card pricing**:
  - PokemonPriceTracker offers Japanese card prices in USD with 1+ year of history and an API — [PokemonPriceTracker](https://www.pokemonpricetracker.com/japanese-pokemon-card-prices)
  - SNKRDUNK publishes transaction-based JP prices and Japanese-shop buylist prices in English — [SNKRDUNK](https://snkrdunk.com/en/magazine/2026/07/14/japanese-pokemon-tcg-market-report-top-50-most-traded-psa-10-cards-first-half-market-report/)

### Inferences
- **The TCGplayer platform is a risk.** TCGplayer owns the marketplace, owns BinderPOS, and sets fees, and it has just raised them. Any sync or pricing tool that depends on TCGplayer data or API access depends on eBay's goodwill. I did not verify current TCGplayer API access terms; that is a gap. The BinderPOS sign-up pause is a small opening: stores that want a Shopify-based POS have to go elsewhere. ShadowPOS, blstr and Synq are already chasing that opening.
- **Gaps I found that still look plausibly open:**
  - (a) Multi-channel inventory sync across TCGplayer, eBay and Shopify, aimed at small online sellers.
  - (b) Grading-submission management for shops that run customer group subs (one fresh competitor with no reviews).
  - (c) Japanese-card pricing and catalog import into Shopify or POS for US stores that sell JP product.
  - (d) Event/tournament management is mostly covered: ShadowPOS bundles it, and Play! Pokémon, Wizards' EventLink and Bandai's tools are free official options. That last point comes from general knowledge and is not verified here.
- **Unit economics.** Stores already pay $100–200/month for POS. A point tool such as grading tracking or JP pricing would probably command $15–50/month. At that price, $1k MRR means about 25–60 paying shops, and $10k MRR means hundreds. That is hard to reach quickly through cold outreach to a fragmented base of LGS owners.
- **Reachability is decent.** Store owners are visible on Google Maps, store locators (Wizards, Play! Pokémon), Discord and Facebook groups, and the many small Shopify TCG stores are easy to find. LGS owners are known to be price-sensitive and thin-margin, which is a general industry view and not sourced here.
- **Founder background helps but does not create a moat.** It is useful for credibility in outreach and for understanding JP product. Competitors such as Grade Flow Tracker are also built by hobbyists.

### Gaps
- No credible count of US local game stores or online card sellers. Industry lore says roughly 4–6k LGS, but I found no source, so do not cite it.
- I could not retrieve Reddit threads (r/lgs, r/mtgfinance) or TCGplayer seller forum threads, so there is no first-hand complaint data. Owner pain points are inferred from product positioning and a single app review.
- Current TCGplayer Pro tier pricing: the help page returned 403. TCGplayer API availability to new developers was not checked.
- Revenue of BinderPOS, ShadowPOS, Crystal Commerce and the Shopify TCG apps is unknown.

## Q2: Japan side: underserved Japan–US B2B workflows, who pays, how much

### Takeaway
Consumer proxy-buying is commoditized: Buyee, ZenMarket and FROM JAPAN charge about ¥300–800 per item. US import economics got worse in 2025: de minimis ended on 2025-08-29, a 15% baseline tariff applies to Japan, and proxies now collect duties up front (DDP). Data tools built on Mercari JP face a ToS ban on commercial use of its data without written permission. B2B sourcing agents for Japanese wholesale (Super Delivery, NETSEA) are a real service, but it is a service business rather than software. Japanese ability is a genuine edge there, but it does not scale easily to fast MRR.

### Cited Findings
- **Proxy fees:**
  - Buyee raised its fee from ¥300 to ¥500 on 2026-04-01.
  - ZenMarket charges ¥300 per item for Recommended Stores and ¥800 for a single Mercari or auction item.
  - FROM JAPAN has charged a flat ¥500 per item since July 2024.
  - Buyee and ZenMarket moved US shipments to duty prepaid (DDP) in August 2025, and Buyee uses category-based duty, about 15% for most goods.
  - All from a third-party comparison site; check against official pages — [Japan Landed](https://japanlanded.pages.dev/articles/buyee-vs-zenmarket/); ZenMarket's official fees page — [ZenMarket](https://www.zenmarket.jp/fees.aspx)
- **Tariffs and de minimis:**
  - The US de minimis exemption ended for all countries on 2025-08-29, and the US-Japan agreement set a baseline 15% tariff. US TCG importers raised prices, and collectors moved toward domestic singles and English-language cards — [CardCodex](https://cardcodex.com/blog/us-tariffs-influence-pokemon-card-hobby/); [JYW Pokeca notice](https://jywpokeca.reamaze.com/kb/important/change-for-us-customers-import-tariff-exemption-ends-august-29-2025); [SNKRDUNK](https://snkrdunk.com/en/magazine/2025/04/04/japanese-tcg-products-to-be-sold-at-higher-prices-in-the-us-following-new-tariff/)
  - Collector forum thread: "buying cards from Japan just got 24% more expensive." The 24% figure was later revised to 15% — [Elite Fourum](https://www.elitefourum.com/t/so-buying-cards-from-japan-just-got-24-more-expensive/55218?page=5)
- **Japanese TCG market:**
  - SNKRDUNK reports a 30th-anniversary surge in JP box prices in 2026 — [SNKRDUNK](https://snkrdunk.com/en/magazine/2026/04/24/the-30th-anniversary-gold-rush-why-japanese-pokemon-boxes-are-hitting-all-time-highs/)
  - A Tokyo retailer described the market in March 2026 as "a correction — not a crash" and noted JP booster prices rising in May 2026 — [Samurai Sword Tokyo](https://samuraiswordtokyo.com/blogs/news/japanese-pokemon-card-market-trends-2026). Both sources are promotional.
- **Mercari JP ToS:** Mercari Japan prohibits using its services, content or data for commercial purposes outside the service without prior written permission — [Mercari JP guide](https://gl.help.jp.mercari.com/en/guide/articles/gl_0053). Third-party scrapers such as Apify's push compliance onto the user — [Apify](https://apify.com/parseforge/mercari-scraper)
- **Wholesale sourcing:**
  - US retailers source Japanese goods through importers, trade shows, direct manufacturer deals, B2B marketplaces, or buying agents and trading companies — [Orosy](https://japanwholesale.orosy.com/blog/how-to-source-japanese-products-wholesale-us)
  - Super Delivery and NETSEA are major Japanese B2B wholesale platforms; watch for hidden membership and logistics fees — [Airwallex](https://www.airwallex.com/en-hk/blog/japan-wholesale)
  - Agents typically provide language help, market insight and vendor verification — [Wise](https://wise.com/us/blog/how-to-find-suppliers-in-japan)
  - The US government notes Japan's B2B e-commerce is expanding into higher-quality consumer goods — [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/japan-b2b-ecommerce)
  - Orosy itself is an incumbent: a Japanese wholesale portal aimed at US retailers (same source).

### Inferences
- **Concrete ideas, ranked** (all "low" weight per the founder's instruction):
  1. **JP-card catalog and pricing feed for US stores and Shopify sellers.** Japanese set names translated and mapped to English equivalents, SNKRDUNK-style comps in USD with tariff and shipping included, and bulk listing into Shopify or TCGplayer. Buyers: US LGS and online sellers who carry JP product. Price: about $20–50/month. Competitors: PokemonPriceTracker (has an API), plus the stores' own manual work. **Risks:** data sourcing. SNKRDUNK and Mercari scraping is ToS-risky; licensed sources or an API would be needed. The tariff has shrunk the JP-import segment.
  2. **Grading-submission tracker for shops**, built TCG-agnostic, with a customer status page. Price: about $15–30/month. Competitors: Grade Flow Tracker ($14.99, launched 2025-09, no reviews), and the grading companies' own apps. Easy to build. The market is small but reachable, since shops that run group subs advertise them publicly.
  3. **Lightweight multi-channel sync for small card sellers** (TCGplayer, eBay, Shopify). Clear demand signal: PowerList and blstr exist, BinderPOS sign-ups are paused, and ShadowPOS lacks TCGplayer and eBay sync. But it depends on TCGplayer and eBay APIs (eBay owns TCGplayer), and it is technically the heaviest of the five. It is not fast to revenue.
  4. **Japanese B2B sourcing desk as a productized service** (Super Delivery and NETSEA ordering, supplier negotiation, inspection) for US boutique and hobby retailers. Price: a percentage of order value or a retainer. This uses Japanese ability directly and could bring first revenue within weeks. But it is labor, not SaaS, and the tariff makes the economics worse for buyers. Orosy and existing agents compete.
  5. **Japanese localization QA for US SaaS and game companies.** This could not be evidenced in this pass (see Gaps). It is a freelance service, and AI translation is compressing rates (general knowledge, unverified).
- **Comparison against general B2B.** None of these shows unusually strong evidence. The TCG boom is real, but the money flows to marketplaces, consumer apps (Collectr's eight-figure ARR is B2C) and big POS vendors. B2B point tools sit at $15–50/month in a fragmented, price-sensitive base. The Japan side carries platform and ToS risk, and the 2025 tariffs worsen the economics. **Verdict: keep these weighted LOW.** The best of them, a grading tracker or a JP-pricing add-on, can serve as a small side bet or a fast validation exercise (about $1–2k MRR is plausible). As a primary path to $10k MRR they look weaker than general B2B niches with larger buyer counts and higher price points.
- **ToS flags:**
  - Mercari JP: commercial use of its data is explicitly barred.
  - SNKRDUNK, Yahoo Auctions JP and Suruga-ya: terms not checked; assume scraping is prohibited.
  - TCGplayer: API access terms were not checked, and eBay controls access.

### Gaps
- Yahoo Auctions JP and Suruga-ya API availability and ToS were not checked. Yahoo Japan's developer documentation was not retrieved.
- Market size and pricing for Japanese localization QA: no sources gathered.
- Revenue of Buyee, ZenMarket or B2B sourcing agents and their published B2B fee schedules: the Orosy fee section was not retrieved.
- No direct evidence (survey or forum) that US LGS owners want JP-card pricing tools. The demand is inferred from the existence of JP-import retailers and price trackers.
