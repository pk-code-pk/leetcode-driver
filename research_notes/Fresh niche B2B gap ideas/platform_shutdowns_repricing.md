# Platform shutdowns, deprecations and repricing (2025–2027) that left small-business users stranded

Scope: new instances beyond those already covered (Shopify Stocky/Scripts, Slack API limits, Atlassian DC end-of-sale, Airtable sync, QuickBooks API metering). Research date: 2026-11-24. About 25 search/fetch calls. Viability scores (1–10) and wedge, outreach and MVP-effort notes are my own inferences for a solo, technical founder with under $100/month budget who wants revenue within weeks. They are not sourced facts.

## Which SaaS tools, apps, plugins, APIs or platform features used by small businesses were sunset, acquired and shut down, or sharply repriced in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
The strongest new instances are Microsoft's run of consumer and SMB app retirements: Publisher (Oct 2026), Lens (Mar 2026), Skype (May 2025) and To Do (announced for Jun 2027). Next come Google's removal of Business Profile Q&A (Dec 2025), Square's wind-down of Weebly in 67 countries (Jun 2026 to Jan 2027), Exchange Online's SMTP AUTH Basic retirement (now pushed into 2027), Make's switch to credits (Aug 2025) and Google's Content API for Shopping sunset (Aug 2026). Several candidates I expected turned out to be reversed or unconfirmed: Amazon SP-API fees, an Etsy Pattern shutdown, eBay Selling Manager Pro, and Eventbrite fee hikes.

### Cited Findings

**1. Microsoft Publisher retired (Oct 2026). Freshest and most clearly stranded group.**
- Microsoft 365 subscribers lose Publisher access on October 1, 2026, and perpetual-license support ends October 13, 2026 (when Office LTSC 2021 and Office 2021 reach end of support). After the cutoff, M365 subscribers can't open or edit .pub files in Publisher — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/publisher/microsoft-publisher-will-no-longer-be-supported-after-october-2026); [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/?p=4224008)
- Microsoft's guidance is to convert files to PDF, including a bulk PowerShell script (`Convert-PubFileToPDF.ps1`). The suggested replacements are Word, PowerPoint and Microsoft Create/Designer — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/publisher/microsoft-publisher-will-no-longer-be-supported-after-october-2026)
- User voice (Microsoft Q&A, 2025-12-14, "Elk_Lady", a nonprofit volunteer): "I am going to be at a loss come October when Publisher expires. I can't find anything as dynamic to create monthly newsletters for the non-profit I belong to. Word is not flexible enough to move text fields, add pictures, rearrange items on the page. PowerPoint is not flexible either. Please reconsider sun-setting Publisher." Also: "I have looked into Canva templates, and they are not as user-friendly as I would like." The newsletter runs about 10 pages a month, printed, folded and mailed by USPS. A Microsoft moderator replied that Microsoft "has not official[ly] announced ... developing a direct replacement for Publisher" — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5660715/is-word-going-to-be-the-only-option-to-create-newl)
- Other Q&A threads ask "can i migrate my publisher files what replaces it" and "is there a replacement program for publisher". Summaries of community posts mention churches (bulletins), museums, and one user with "thousands of documents" in Publisher — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5780232/can-i-migrate-my-publisher-files-what-replaces-it); [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ca/answers/questions/5702013/is-there-a-replacement-program-for-publisher)
- Third-party alternatives named: Affinity Publisher, Scribus, Canva and LibreOffice Draw. LibreOffice Draw opens .pub files directly, but its handling of complex layouts, tables and WordArt is described as "flaky" — [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/?p=4224008)
- Universities (W&M, WashU, ODU, UW) ran their own retirement notices, which suggests the installed base was broad — [W&M IT](https://www.wm.edu/offices/it/announcements/microsoft-publisher-retiring-oct-2026.php); [WashU IT](https://it.washu.edu/2026/08/03/microsoft-publisher-is-retiring/)
- *Wedge (inference):* a "Publisher Rescue" web app. It would bulk-convert .pub archives into *editable* formats (Scribus .sla, IDML, DOCX with positioned frames, or Canva-importable PDF/SVG), using LibreOffice/libmspub headless plus cleanup, and it could add a simple print-newsletter template editor aimed at churches and nonprofits. *Reach:* Microsoft Q&A threads (read-only research; any posting should be the founder's own call), church-administrator and parish-secretary communities, diocesan and denominational offices, small-museum associations, and SEO on "open .pub file without Publisher". *MVP:* 1–2 weeks. *Pricing idea:* $15–49 one-time per archive, or a $5–9/mo template editor. *Risk:* free converters exist (LibreOffice, Zamzar), demand is mostly one-time, and the audience is older and less online. *Score: 6.5/10* (the pain is fresh, the date is fixed, the users are clearly upset and nothing replaces Publisher directly).

**2. Microsoft To Do: retirement announced for June 30, 2027 (prep window).**
- The To Do apps retire on June 30, 2027. Work/school tasks won't migrate to Planner automatically; they stay reachable through Planner until the same date. Personal Microsoft accounts, including M365 Personal and Family, get no Planner path, and Microsoft tells them to pick another app and prepare to export. Export instructions haven't been published yet. Planner has no native Windows or Mac desktop apps, no list groups, and no attachments in private plans, and no printing or email-list features are planned. User comments: "What a mess." and "Not everything needs to be put into an AI/Copilot agent." — [The Register, 2026-11-19](https://theregister.com/personal-tech/2026/11/19/microsoft-to-do-will-soon-become-microsoft-done/5310973)
- Headlines: "Microsoft Is Shutting Down To Do in 2027, Leaving Users Scrambling for Alternatives" — [Windows Report](https://windowsreport.com/microsoft-is-shutting-down-to-do-in-2027-leaving-users-scrambling-for-alternatives); "Microsoft To Do is closing down and personal users are being abandoned" — [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/microsoft-to-do-is-closing-down); "Microsoft is killing its great tasks app in favor of its worse tasks app" — [TechBytes](https://techbytes.app/posts/microsoft-is-killing-its-great-tasks-app-in-favor-of/)
- *Wedge (inference):* a one-click "To Do Exporter" that pulls lists, steps, notes, due dates and recurrence through the Microsoft Graph To Do API and pushes them into Todoist, TickTick, Things, Apple Reminders, Notion or CSV. Alternatively, a small To Do-style app for solo businesses with a native importer. *Reach:* SEO and Product Hunt timed to Microsoft's export announcement; Reddit threads (read-only research); affiliate deals with destination apps. *MVP:* 1–2 weeks. *Risk:* mostly consumers, so willingness to pay is low ($5–15 one-time); destination apps may ship free importers; Microsoft may ship its own export. *Score: 6/10* (huge base and a known deadline, but low prices; best as a fast cash-flow tool plus affiliate revenue).

**3. Exchange Online SMTP AUTH Basic auth retirement: delayed into 2027 (prep window for MSPs).**
- The original plan was to start rejecting Basic-auth SMTP submissions on March 1, 2026, with full rejection on April 30, 2026. Microsoft put that on hold in a January 27, 2026 update. The current reading: no behavior change through December 2026; new tenants created after December 2026 get Basic auth off by default; a final removal date will be announced in the second half of 2027. One third-party summary instead says existing tenants get default-off at the end of December 2026, so sources disagree — [Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/exchange/updated-exchange-online-smtp-auth-basic-authentication-deprecation-timeline/4489835); [Tech Community discussion](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/exchange_general/microsoft-delays-retirement-of-basic-authentication-for-smtp-auth/4490414/replies/4491594)
- The affected devices are "older devices such as printers, scanners, monitoring systems, or legacy LOB applications" using Basic auth for SMTP submission. Vendors suggest third-party SMTP relays because scanners often support only Basic SMTP auth — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5645009/exchange-online-to-retire-basic-auth-for-client-su); [SMTP2GO blog](https://www.smtp2go.com/blog/the-end-of-microsofts-basic-authentication-for-smtp-auth/)
- *Wedge (inference):* a hosted "scan-to-email bridge". MFPs and legacy apps keep Basic SMTP (or SMTP with a per-device password) to the bridge, which delivers through the tenant's Microsoft Graph `sendMail` with OAuth admin consent, so mail still comes from the tenant's own mailbox and passes SPF/DMARC. Sell to MSPs at about $3–5 per device per month, with an audit report built from the Exchange admin center's SMTP AUTH client report. *Reach:* MSP communities and copier/MFP dealers (cold email); the founder's own posts only. *MVP:* 2–3 weeks. *Risk:* general relays (SMTP2GO, SendGrid, Microsoft's own connector relay and High Volume Email) already exist; Microsoft keeps moving the date; email abuse and deliverability liability; the security bar is high when you hold OAuth tokens. *Score: 5.5/10.*

### Inferences
- The richest vein for 2025–27 is Microsoft retiring small "boring" utilities (Publisher, Lens, Skype, To Do) and steering users to Copilot, OneDrive or Planner. Each time, the replacement lacks a specific workflow (positioned-frame print layout, business-card capture, list groups and personal accounts). Microsoft rarely builds converters, so a solo "rescue/export" tool can win in a fixed window.
- "Export/convert before the deadline" tools are the fastest to revenue (1–2 week MVPs, one-time payments, SEO demand that peaks on a known date) but weakest for MRR. A converter funnel into a small subscription (Publisher → newsletter template editor; To Do → simple task app; SMTP audit → per-device relay) is the realistic route to $1–10k MRR.
- Ranking for this founder: (1) Publisher Rescue, now, demand live; (2) To Do Exporter, build now, launch when Microsoft publishes export guidance; (3) Exchange SMTP scan-to-email bridge for MSPs, B2B recurring revenue, timed for the 2027 cutoff; (4) GBP Q&A/Ask Maps readiness for local-SEO agencies.
- Platform/ToS risk flags: Google Maps scraping (#6), Microsoft Graph OAuth admin-consent and token custody (#3), Microsoft Graph To Do API rate limits and Microsoft possibly shipping its own export (#2), telecom regulation (#8).

### Gaps
- No quantified size for any affected group: Microsoft discloses no Publisher, Lens or To Do user counts; Weebly's site count in the 67 countries is unknown; the number of devices using Exchange SMTP AUTH Basic is unknown.
- Replacement pricing wasn't verified from primary sources (Affinity, Canva Pro, SMTP2GO/SendGrid tiers, Teams Essentials, Todoist/TickTick). Check before pricing a wedge.
- The Windows Central Lens article and the Square Community Weebly thread wouldn't load, so the direct user quotes for #4 and #5 come from headlines only.
- I didn't directly search Zapier 2025–26 pricing, Google Workspace/Microsoft 365 2026 price increases, Squarespace/Acuity price hikes, Workplace from Meta's 2026 shutdown, Meta Page Insights metric deprecations, X API pay-per-use pricing, or WordPress plugin abandonments. All are open leads for another pass.
- Mailchimp's free-plan automation removal date and Booker's real status need primary sources.

## Are there upcoming 2027 sunsets already announced (a window to prepare)?

### Takeaway
Yes. Most relevant to a solo founder: Microsoft To Do (June 30, 2027), Exchange Online SMTP AUTH Basic (default-off for new tenants after Dec 2026; final date to be announced in H2 2027) and the last Weebly account access in 67 countries (Dec 26, 2026 / Jan 2, 2027). Other 2027 dates are enterprise or infrastructure (Windows Server 2016 on Jan 12, 2027; vSphere 8 on Oct 11, 2027; SAP ECC and SAP IDM at end of 2027) or consumer (Google Nest app on Jan 25, 2027) and are poor fits.
