# Sharetribe leads a field with no clear winner

No open-source repository on GitHub today ships the full TaskRabbit loop (post a task, browse and book providers, two separate profiles, messaging, two-way reviews and payouts) as a codebase that is all four of maintained, permissively licensed, self-hostable and substantial. Every serious candidate fails at least one of those tests. **Sharetribe Go** (Rails 8.1, ~118k LOC) has the most complete feature set you can self-host, but it is source-available rather than open source and officially "no longer actively maintained" ([README](https://github.com/sharetribe/sharetribe)). The **Sharetribe Web Template** (Apache-2.0, ~156k LOC, active) delivers the best TaskRabbit-shaped user experience, but it is only a frontend and depends on Sharetribe's paid hosted backend, which costs **$199/month billed yearly** to go live ([pricing](https://www.sharetribe.com/pricing/)). **Cocorico** (MIT, Symfony, ~91k LOC) is a full service-booking marketplace that has been frozen since 2021. If you want code you fully own on a modern stack, the strongest bases are **Spree 5** (BSD-3, marketplace with Stripe Connect payouts in core) or **Mercur** (MIT, TypeScript on Medusa), each of which still needs booking, messaging and two-way reviews added. On the jobs side, **PeelJobs** (Django, MIT, ~53k LOC) is the most complete two-sided job board but has no payments or reviews. The student-specific gig projects, **Hustl** and **CampusGigs**, model a students-to-gigs product almost exactly, but each is a 2026 solo build with almost no stars or tests. My recommendation: pick Sharetribe for the fastest launch, Spree or Mercur for a platform you own outright, and borrow Hustl's data model in either case.

*Method caveat: the research team could not reach GitHub's per-repo API, so contributor counts and exact commit dates are missing. Stars, forks, last-push dates and licenses come from GitHub search. Line counts (LOC) are approximate non-blank lines from shallow clones. Nobody ran any of the apps, so feature claims rest on READMEs, schemas and code searches.*

## Three ownership models split the serious candidates

The candidates fall into three groups, and the group you choose decides your trade-off more than any single feature does. The first group is **complete marketplace engines** (Sharetribe, Cocorico, Agriya Getlancer). They already include listings, transactions, booking, messaging, reviews and payments. A grep across the clones makes the gap concrete: Sharetribe Go has **541 files** that touch booking or transactions, the Web Template 285 and Cocorico 159, while job boards such as PeelJobs have only 0 to 6 ([sharetribe](https://github.com/sharetribe/sharetribe), [cocorico](https://github.com/Cocolabs-SAS/cocorico), [PeelJobs](https://github.com/MicroPyramid/opensource-job-portal)). The second group is **multi-vendor commerce engines** (Spree, Mercur, Medusa). They are actively maintained and permissively licensed, and they handle seller onboarding, commissions and Stripe Connect payouts well, but they are built around products and carts rather than services. The third group is **job boards and applicant tracking systems** (PeelJobs, SpotAxis, django-job-portal, OpenCATS). They match the "students and employers" framing and give you strong profiles, postings and applications, but they lack the entire transactional core of a gig marketplace.

## Sharetribe comes closest to TaskRabbit, at the cost of license or lock-in

**Sharetribe Go** is the most feature-complete self-hostable codebase. It is a 12-year-old Rails app with roughly 19,572 commits and RSpec, Cucumber and Selenium test suites. It runs on Ruby 3.4.8, Rails 8.1.2 and MySQL 8.4, and supports Stripe in the self-hosted version (PayPal works only on Sharetribe-hosted sites) ([README](https://github.com/sharetribe/sharetribe/blob/master/README.md)). Two problems weigh against it. First, its README opens with "**Sharetribe Go is no longer actively maintained**," and Sharetribe says it will eventually sunset the product without having given a date ([community post](https://www.sharetribe.com/community/t/sharetribe-go-is-no-longer-actively-maintained-discover-the-new-sharetribe/3754)). The November 2026 Rails upgrade shows that dependencies are still being bumped, but this is upkeep rather than product development. Second, the project moved from MIT to a source-available license in 2019 ([Sharetribe blog](https://sharetribe.com/balanced/sharetribe-go-becomes-source-available)). The Sharetribe Community Public License lets you run and modify your own marketplace. It does not let you host the software "for multiple clients" in a way that competes with Sharetribe, and it is governed by Finnish law ([LICENSE](https://github.com/sharetribe/sharetribe/blob/master/LICENSE)). A single student-gig marketplace that you operate is allowed. A white-label campus-marketplace SaaS sold to many universities is not. Its data model is also built around providers posting listings that customers book, not around clients posting tasks that taskers bid on, so a "post a task, receive offers" flow would need custom work.

The **Sharetribe Web Template** maps almost one-to-one onto tasker-and-client gigs. It supports hourly calendar availability up to a year ahead, booking requests the provider accepts, messaging threaded into the transaction timeline, and reviews that stay hidden until both parties have reviewed or the review window closes. Payouts run through Stripe Connect Custom accounts ([payments overview](https://www.sharetribe.com/docs/concepts/payments/payments-overview/), [transaction engine](https://www.sharetribe.com/features/transaction-engine.md)). The code itself is Apache-2.0, but the backend, database and business logic live on Sharetribe's servers. No self-hosted backend exists. Pricing is **$39/month for test mode only**, then $199/month billed yearly (or $259 monthly) to process real transactions ([pricing](https://www.sharetribe.com/pricing/)). For a student startup, about $2.4k a year is affordable. The larger cost is that every custom behaviour has to fit Sharetribe's transaction-process model, and your data lives on its servers. (The reported 69 stars against 280 forks looks like a scraping or metadata artifact and should not be read as low adoption.)

**Cocorico** is the only MIT-licensed full service-booking marketplace of real size, but it is effectively dead. Its last push was in October 2021, it requires PHP ^7.1, Symfony ^3.4 and the deprecated ext-mongo driver, and its roadmap still lists Symfony 2.x deprecation fixes ([composer.json](https://github.com/Cocolabs-SAS/cocorico/blob/master/composer.json), [ROADMAP](https://github.com/Cocolabs-SAS/cocorico/blob/master/ROADMAP.md)). Its makers moved the effort into a closed commercial product, Second ([repo](https://github.com/Cocolabs-SAS/cocorico)). **Agriya's Getlancer Quote** was explicitly built as a "Thumbtack, Zaarly, Redbeacon, TaskRabbit" clone and was once sold as a commercial script. It is now under an OSL-3.0 plus commercial dual license, with GitHub detecting no license file, written in AngularJS, and untouched since 2018 ([Getlancer README](https://github.com/agriya/getlancerv3-bidding)). It is worth reading as a reference for the request-for-quote flow, not as a base. **Algora** (Elixir/Phoenix, AGPL-3.0, 1,543 stars) is a live two-sided hiring and bounty platform with Stripe payouts, but it targets software engineers, and its network-copyleft license would require you to publish your modifications ([README](https://github.com/algora-io/algora)).

## Spree and Mercur offer code you own, but in the wrong shape

If owning your code outright matters more than speed, the multi-vendor commerce engines are the strongest maintained foundations. **Spree 5** puts its marketplace features in the BSD-licensed core: seller onboarding checklists, per-seller order splitting, a commission engine, a payout ledger with Stripe Connect and a Seller Panel. It pairs a Rails backend with a Next.js 16 / React 19 storefront, and it has 15.8k stars and over 30k commits ([GitHub](https://github.com/spree/spree)). **Mercur** is the TypeScript equivalent. Built on Medusa, it provides a seller panel, an admin console, a buyer storefront and a Stripe Connect payout provider under an MIT core, with end-to-end and integration test suites ([GitHub](https://github.com/mercurjs/mercur)). Mercur markets payments, compliance and settlement as part of its paid Enterprise tier, which conflicts with the README's payout provider, so check the docs before relying on its open-source tier for production money movement ([mercurjs.com](https://www.mercurjs.com/)). Bare **Medusa** (37.3k stars) offers only a recipe for building a marketplace yourself, and **Saleor** has no multi-vendor support at all ([Medusa recipe](https://docs.medusajs.com/resources/recipes/marketplace), [Saleor](https://github.com/saleor/saleor)).

The problem with both is shape. They model "a seller lists a product, a buyer checks out a cart." A gig needs "a task request, then a quote or acceptance, then scheduling, then completion, then payout release." None of the reviewed sources confirm booking, buyer-seller messaging or two-way reviews in either engine, so you would build those yourself. For scheduling, **Cal.diy** is now MIT-licensed (relicensed from AGPL in April 2026 when Cal.com took its production code private), but its own README says it is "strictly recommended for personal, non-production use" ([Cal.com blog](https://cal.com/pt-BR/blog/cal-diy-open-source-to-closed-source), [GitHub](https://github.com/calcom/cal.com)). Treat it as code to borrow ideas from, not as a dependency. Cal.com's own experts-marketplace starter kit was archived in March 2025 ([GitHub](https://github.com/calcom/platform-starter-kit)). The research also found no complete open-source Next.js plus Supabase service marketplace ([search summary](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Frofiyev%2Fdilshod-ui)).

## Job boards cover the student side but lack the gig core

For the "students and employers" half of the brief, **PeelJobs** is the most complete maintained two-sided job platform. It has candidate profiles (skills, education, employment history, projects, certifications, resumes), recruiter and company-admin roles, job posting and application management, Google OAuth, job alerts and a `UserMessage` messaging model. It runs on Django 4.2 with PostgreSQL full-text search, Celery and Redis, has a separate SvelteKit recruiter app, and includes docs, BDD tests and Docker ([README](https://github.com/MicroPyramid/opensource-job-portal/blob/master/README.md), [models](https://github.com/MicroPyramid/opensource-job-portal/tree/master/backend/peeldb)). It has no payments, reviews or booking. **SpotAxis** has the broadest feature set (113k LOC: candidate CVs, vacancies, applications with scoring, messaging, interview scheduling, resume parsing and a payments app), but its payments are employer subscriptions to the platform rather than payouts to workers. Its README still lists Python 2.7 while `pyproject.toml` requires Python 3.12 and Django 5.2 ([README](https://github.com/Assystant/SpotAxis/blob/master/README.md), [pyproject.toml](https://github.com/Assystant/SpotAxis/blob/master/pyproject.toml)). **django-job-portal** (625 stars, ~8.6k LOC, CI, Docker) is a clean starter but small. **OpenCATS** and **YAWIK** are recruiter-only applicant tracking systems with almost no candidate side ([OpenCATS](https://github.com/opencats/OpenCATS), [YAWIK](https://github.com/cross-solution/YAWIK)). **railsdevs.com** (MIT, archived, 192 test files) ran as a real business with developer profiles, paid business subscriptions to message candidates, conversations and hiring agreements through Stripe. That makes it a valuable production reference for the profile-plus-messaging side ([models](https://github.com/joemasilotti/railsdevs.com/tree/main/app/models)).

Student-specific projects exist, but none is mature. **nijobs** from the University of Porto was the most rigorous campus job board, with Jest tests and Codecov coverage. It is GPL-3.0, archived since 2024, and has no student accounts or applications in its backend ([nijobs-be](https://github.com/NIAEFEUP/nijobs-be)). **Hustl** has the schema closest to a students-to-gigs platform found anywhere. Its 28 Prisma models include StudentProfile, BusinessProfile, Listing, Match, ChatMessage, Review, ShiftCheckIn, DisputeThread, Wallet, PayoutRequest and College ([schema](https://github.com/ankan00V/Hustl/blob/main/prisma/schema.prisma)). Its README claims PostGIS geosearch, Razorpay escrow and match scoring, but it is a 3-star solo project with about five test files ([README](https://github.com/ankan00V/Hustl)). **CampusGigs** (MIT, Next.js plus Express plus Flutter) models gigs, applications, reviews, chat and universities, but has no payments, and its README is still create-next-app boilerplate ([migrations](https://github.com/Kanjo-Elkamira-Ndi/CampusGigs/tree/main/migrations)). Several 2026 repos in this space, including CampusGigs, freelancezero and SkillShift, contain AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md files. Their large LOC counts therefore say little about how battle-tested they are. **SkillShift** (escrow ledger, reviews gated to completed orders, but payments "intentionally simulated") and **freelancezero** (a rich Upwork-style contract and milestone schema at "v0.1 MVP foundation") are similar reference-grade code ([SkillShift](https://github.com/adityasah15/skillshift), [freelancezero](https://github.com/freelancezero/freelancezero)). Almost every repo tagged taskrabbit-clone, fiverr-clone or upwork-clone is either a bootcamp project with fewer than 40 stars and no license, or a README-only advertisement for a paid clone script. A repo with no license grants no reuse rights.

## Recommended starting points, by priority

**Fastest to a real launch: the Sharetribe Web Template.** Configure listing types for "student services," add student and employer fields, and adjust the transaction process to a request, accept, complete, payout flow. Booking, messaging, double-blind reviews and Stripe Connect payouts are already there. Choose it if about $200 a month and vendor lock-in are acceptable and you want to test demand within weeks.

**Best fully owned production base: Spree 5 (Rails plus Next.js) or Mercur (all TypeScript).** Both are actively maintained and permissively licensed, and both already handle the hardest regulated piece: multi-party Stripe Connect payouts with commissions. Plan to build the gig workflow, a scheduling layer, messaging and two-way reviews. That is a substantial project, and the notes give no sourced estimate of its size. Pick between them by your team's language.

**Self-hosted with everything included, if the license fits: Sharetribe Go.** It is the most complete feature set you can run on your own servers, legal for a single marketplace you operate, and current on Rails 8.1. Accept that you will be maintaining it yourself before long, and do not choose it if you might ever resell the platform to other campuses.

**Jobs-first variant: PeelJobs.** If the product is closer to Handshake (internships and part-time jobs with applications) than to TaskRabbit (paid short tasks), PeelJobs gives you two rich profile types and messaging on a maintained, MIT-licensed Django stack. You would add Stripe Connect, reviews and a task-completion state machine.

**Design reference, whichever base you pick: Hustl's schema**, plus railsdevs for paid employer messaging and Getlancer Quote for the post-a-request-and-receive-quotes flow. Avoid Cocorico unless your team can upgrade a PHP 7.1 / Symfony 3.4 codebase, and avoid AGPL or OSL code (Algora, Agriya) in a closed commercial product.

## Conclusion

The gap is structural rather than accidental. Each company that once built a full open-source service marketplace (Sharetribe, Cocolabs, Agriya) later moved its complete product behind SaaS fees or a commercial license. Meanwhile the healthy open-source marketplace projects grew out of e-commerce, which is why payouts are a solved problem in open source while booking-and-reviews service flows are not. For a students-to-gigs founder, that changes the question from "which clone do I fork?" to "which hard part do I want handed to me?" Sharetribe hands you the gig workflow and keeps the backend. Spree and Mercur hand you money movement and leave the workflow to you. The job boards hand you profiles and little else.

A practical implication follows. The most defensible parts of a student marketplace (verification by university, check-in and completion proof, reputation, disputes) are exactly what no mature codebase provides, and only Hustl attempts them. Whichever base you choose, budget engineering time for that trust layer rather than for listings and profiles, which every candidate already handles. Before you commit, spend a day running the two or three finalists. No repository in this report was executed, so setup pain and real feature completeness remain the biggest unknowns.
