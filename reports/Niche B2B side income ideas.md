# Sell the gaps platforms just left behind

The best bet for a solo engineer who wants B2B money soon is a narrow Shopify app for **receiving and dated cost history**. Shopify shut down its free Stocky inventory app on **31 Aug 2026**, and merchants say publicly that the replacement apps cover purchase orders but leave receiving and cost tracking "far less covered" ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/stocky-is-ending-what-workflow-are-you-most-concerned-about-replacing/672546)). It is the only idea in this research backed by direct, dated complaints from buyers. The runner-up makes money faster but rests on weaker evidence: a done-for-you "missed-call text-back + review request" service for one local trade, which comparable operators sell at **$300–800/month per client** ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/building-a-profitable-ai-voice-saas-agency-300-800-mrr-per-client-frAbgO1yQMfHOFFtY3gE)). Third is a single-vertical directory whose listees pay for featured spots. You can sell those spots by cold email within weeks, the way JobBoardSearch made **$1,307.50 in its first paid month** ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/1-307-50-in-revenue-since-launched-job-board-search-paid-stuff-today-exactly-1-month-ago-312de281c4)). The shortlist has 12 ideas. The two trading-card/Japan ideas sit near the bottom, because the evidence for them is not unusually strong. On timing: a first dollar within weeks is realistic, but even the documented indie wins took **about 2–12 months to reach $1–3k MRR** ([BigGo summary of Starter Story](https://finance.biggo.com/podcast/b1cea12aa7641c22)). Plan for $1k MRR in a quarter, not a month. Nearly every revenue figure below is self-reported by founders. Reddit and Capterra review pages were mostly unreachable during research, so most demand evidence is indirect: paid niche tools exist, and incumbents get complaints about price.

## Seven weighted criteria turn "money soon" into a score

Each idea is scored 1–5 on seven criteria. The scores are weighted and converted to a 100-point scale. The weights follow the stated priority, which is cash soon on a budget under $100/month.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 5 means |
|---|---|---|
| Speed to first dollar | 25% | Can be sold, or pre-sold as a service, within 2–4 weeks |
| Demand evidence | 20% | Direct, dated buyer complaints or verified paid comparables, not opinion pieces |
| Reachability by cold outreach | 15% | Buyers can be listed and contacted directly (public lists, Maps, forum threads) |
| Price and path to $1–10k MRR | 15% | Price high enough that 10–30 customers reach $1k+ MRR |
| Build fit | 10% | MVP in 1–3 weeks for one engineer, running costs under $100/month |
| Competitive room | 10% | A real wedge exists against named incumbents |
| Risk (inverted) | 5% | Little platform, terms-of-service, legal or churn exposure |

Ties go to the idea with higher speed, then better build fit. Trading-card and Japan ideas get an explicit **−10 penalty**, as requested, because none showed unusually strong evidence.

Read the evidence with some skepticism. The demand research could not reach Indie Hackers post pages, Upwork research, Reddit, or Capterra review text, so it contains **no gig-volume data and almost no first-hand "I'd pay for this" threads**. Saturation claims are also opinion-based, for example a 2026 "ideas to avoid" list ([WeAreFounders](https://www.wearefounders.uk/10-saas-ideas-to-avoid-as-an-indie-hacker-in-2026/)). Vertical-SaaS pricing often comes from roundups that competing vendors wrote. The Shopify Stocky threads are the exception. In them, merchants list the workflows they are losing in their own words.

A generic outreach benchmark helps calibrate expectations. **100 cold outreaches typically produce 8–15 calls, 3–5 trials, and 1–3 paying customers in month one.** The same source frames $0 to $10k MRR as an **8–12 month** path ([earlycustomers.com](https://earlycustomers.com/resources/founder-playbook/)). The founder-reported best cases fit that picture. Bosley earned **$100 MRR in month one** from cold DMs on X and took about 6 months to reach $1k ([BigGo/Starter Story](https://finance.biggo.com/podcast/b1cea12aa7641c22)). One cold-email SaaS reached $5k MRR in 120 days, but that was in 2023 ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-i-took-my-saas-from-0-to-5k-mrr-in-120-days-using-cold-emails-d4f5f56b45)).

### 1. Shopify receiving and dated-cost ledger, the best-evidenced gap (78)

**Customer:** Shopify merchants with physical retail, many SKUs, or frequent supplier deliveries who used Stocky. **Twist:** don't build another "Stocky replacement" purchase-order tool. Build only the parts that the purchase-order apps skip, and make it work alongside whichever purchase-order app the merchant chose. That means partial and short/damaged receipts, ordered→invoiced→received→final-cost reconciliation, and a **cost snapshot on every receipt**, which gives true historical margins. Merchants point out that "Shopify keeps one cost per variant — the last one you paid", so past margins silently change. They also cite inventory adjustment history capped at 180 days, viewable one variant at a time, and an activity log capped at 250 rows ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/stocky-is-ending-what-workflow-are-you-most-concerned-about-replacing/672546)).

**Demand evidence:** this is the strongest in the corpus. Merchants say the native replacement "drops the parts I cared about most," that native purchase orders take "several times longer than Stocky did," and that alternatives treat receiving as "tap the quantity, done" ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/with-stocky-shutting-down-i-ve-been-trying-to-figure-out-what-to-use-instead/673369); [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/stocky-is-ending-what-workflow-are-you-most-concerned-about-replacing/672546)). **Competitors:** ShelfSprint $12–49/mo, Restock Forecast free to 50 products then $19.99/mo, Provido free (it does not record invoices or per-delivery costs), and Binly, BuyAhead, EZstock and Replenly with no published prices. At least one solo developer has already launched a general replacement ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/self-promotion/shopify-is-killing-stocky-on-august-31st-i-built-the-replacement-launching-on-ph-tomorrow)).

**Price:** $29–49/month flat, positioned above the cheap purchase-order apps because it protects margin data. Shopify takes **0% of the first $1M of lifetime revenue** ([Shopify docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share)). **First 10 customers:** reply in the public Stocky threads and DM their participants (check forum rules on self-promotion first). Then cold-email multi-location Shopify retailers. Offer a founding price in exchange for a review, because reviews are the bottleneck for new Shopify apps. One solo app sat at about $30 MRR for lack of them ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/just-pushed-a-major-update-to-my-shopify-app-would-love-your-feedback-pmd5pFLNkqrtdZxZorga)). **MVP:** an inventory audit-log archiver and exporter takes 1–2 weeks (webhooks, a continuous archive, and CSV or Sheets export). The receiving and cost ledger adds 3–5 weeks.

**Risks:** demand driven by the deadline peaked in Aug–Sep 2026, so the remaining buyers are merchants who are unhappy with a stopgap they chose in a hurry. App Store install counts and ratings for the competitors were not verified. Shopify could also ship native cost history. Upside: Shopify apps can grow well beyond side income. Zigpoll reports $125k MRR solo, about a third of it from the App Store ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/c4o2Tfs6mjdpip5yZhaO)). That is an outlier.

### 2. Done-for-you trade front office, the fastest cash with the weakest proof (77)

**Customer:** one trade at a time, such as septic, HVAC or garage doors, with owner-run shops at roughly $500K–5M revenue. **Twist:** sell a narrow, done-for-you outcome to one vertical: every missed call gets an instant text-back, and every finished job triggers a review request. Run it on your own scripts first and productize later. Don't sell a horizontal review tool. The template comes from an Indie Hackers operator who sells AI receptionist plus automation bundles for **$1–2k setup + $300–800/month**, claiming about 80% margin ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/building-a-profitable-ai-voice-saas-agency-300-800-mrr-per-client-frAbgO1yQMfHOFFtY3gE)).

**Evidence:** that single, undated post, plus the general pattern that indie products repositioned as outcomes ("leads on autopilot") grow faster ([BigGo/Starter Story](https://finance.biggo.com/podcast/b1cea12aa7641c22)). **Competitors:** funded review platforms such as Partoo, which added AI review analysis in 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partoo)), plus countless AI-automation agencies. **Price:** about $1k setup and $199–399/month, so **4–8 clients reach $1–3k MRR**. That is the smallest customer count on this list. **First 10:** cold-call shops from Google Maps in one metro and lead with a free audit of how many of their calls went unanswered. **MVP:** 1–2 weeks of SMS and webhook glue. To stay under $100/month, start with SMS text-back, not voice AI, because telephony costs grow with each customer.

**Risks:** high churn is common in AI-agency offers, though no source quantified it. US business texting needs carrier registration, so check that before selling. And this is closer to an agency than to SaaS. It wins on speed and loses on evidence.

### 3. A featured-listing directory for one B2B vertical (68)

**Customer:** vendors selling into one vertical that G2 and Capterra serve poorly, for example tools for Webflow agencies or for commercial cleaning companies. A variant is a directory of B2B newsletters and communities that accept sponsors, in which case the listees and advertisers are the customers. **Twist:** structured comparison data such as pricing tier and integrations, plus a weekly "new tools" email, with listings sold by outreach rather than waiting for SEO.

**Evidence:** OpenAlternative reports **$57k gross in 2025 and about $6k MRR**, with featured listings at $197/month. It was built in 48 hours but took about a year to reach meaningful revenue ([Dirstarter](https://dirstarter.com/blog/how-much-do-directory-websites-make)). SaaSHub makes $10k+ MRR from about 100 featured listings at ~$99/month, which took years (same source). JobBoardSearch shows how quickly a directory whose listees are themselves businesses can make money: $1.3k in its first paid month ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/1-307-50-in-revenue-since-launched-job-board-search-paid-stuff-today-exactly-1-month-ago-312de281c4)). **Price:** $49–149/month per featured vendor. **First 10:** cold-email the 50–100 vendors you list and offer founding featured spots. **MVP:** 1–2 weekends at $10–40/month.

**Risks:** the vendor that published the time-to-revenue estimate (3–6 months) sells directory boilerplates. Directories resell at only **0.2–0.7× revenue** on Flippa ([Flippa](https://flippa.com/51080-profitable-established-web-directory)). Whether a given vertical already has a directory was not checked. The SEO side is slow and exposed to AI Overviews, which cut position-one clicks on informational queries by about **58%** in Ahrefs' Dec 2025 data ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/)).

### 4. Narrow tech-stack lead list (68)

**Customer:** agencies and app vendors who sell to users of one platform or plugin. **Twist:** go deeper and cheaper on a single ecosystem than BuiltWith, with fields like install date, plan tier and store-size signals, and detect only from public, logged-out HTML. **Evidence:** willingness to pay is proven by Store Leads at $75/month (G2 4.9/5) and BuiltWith at $295/$495/$995/month, and BuiltWith's reviewers complain most about accuracy ([G2](https://g2.com/products/store-leads/pricing); [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/builtwith)). **Price:** $29–99/month or one-off CSVs. **First 10:** the buyers are other founders and agencies on LinkedIn and X, the audience that dominates indie revenue boards. **MVP:** 2–4 weeks.

**Risks:** Store Leads is already cheap and broad, so the niche needs fields it lacks. Keep the data at company level only. A French regulator fined the contact-data tool KASPR **€240,000** over scraped personal data ([CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/en/data-scraping-kaspr-fined-eu240000)). The 2024 Meta v. Bright Data ruling favoured logged-out scraping, but it rests on Meta's specific contract wording ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/court-rules-in-favor-of-a-web-scraper-bright-data-which-meta-had-used-and-then-sued)).

### 5. Backflow testers: a required interval that produces a required form (65)

**Customer:** certified backflow testers, often solo plumbers or 1–5 person firms. **Twist:** map test results onto each city's or water utility's required form or portal, and email property owners an annual reminder so the tester gets the repeat job. The pattern matters: a legally required recurring test produces a document someone else demands, and spreadsheets and generic field-service software handle that poorly. **Competitors:** Syncta, owned by valve maker Watts, from $29/user/month. Syncta also sells to water utilities, which may put off independent testers. C3Backflow also competes ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/208106/Syncta/); [Syncta purveyors](https://www.syncta.com/water-purveyors)). One reviewer still needs QuickBooks alongside Syncta ([Capterra IE](https://www.capterra.ie/software/208106/syncta)).

**Evidence:** moderate and indirect. Paid tools exist, but no operator complaints were retrieved. **Price:** $39–59/month flat per company. **First 10:** cold-call testers in one metro, using state or utility lists of certified testers if they are public (unverified). **MVP:** 2–4 weeks for device records, PDF generation for 1–3 jurisdictions, and reminders. First customer in roughly 3–6 weeks. **Risks:** forms vary by jurisdiction, so growth is jurisdiction by jurisdiction, and getting from 1k to 10k MRR means expanding to more cities.

### 6–10. Credible but slower or smaller

**6. Apify actor for an under-served B2B source (63).** Buyers are data users on Apify Store. The twist is picking a source with few actors, such as procurement portals, trade-show exhibitor lists or plugin marketplaces, and alerting on zero-result runs, since silent failures drove churn in a documented case. Developers keep 80%, and top creators make $10k+ MRR ([Apify](https://help.apify.com/en/articles/8684010-how-does-actor-monetization-work)). But one realistic new actor made **about $200/month net after 3 months**, and the author puts typical paid actors at $50–500/month ([DEV](https://dev.to/boo_n/3-months-shipping-my-first-apify-actor-64-users-200mo-and-everything-i-got-wrong-52i7)). The build takes days, and the marketplace supplies the buyers. That is the quickest true first dollar, but the ceiling is low unless you build a portfolio of actors. Hot sources such as new business filings already have 12+ actors at $1.25–10 per 1k records ([scrapemint](https://apify.com/scrapemint/new-business-registration-leads)).

**7. Flat-price florist proposals (62).** Buyers are wedding and event florists. The twist is no event caps, plus automatic wholesaler order sheets and recipe costing. A Capterra reviewer said Details Flowers "skyrockets to $150 per month once you go over 10 events total" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/175475/Details-Flowers-Software/pricing/)). Competitors: Curate ~$275/user (unverified), RoseCore.AI $45 flat, Floranext $29.99 per feature ([Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1203041/RoseCore)). RoseCore already pitches "no per-wedding charges," so the twist has to go further. Price at $39–49/month. Florists are active on Instagram, so DMs work. Build takes 3–4 weeks. Demand is seasonal, so do outreach in the off-season.

**8. Fire-extinguisher tagging (61).** Buyers are single-van extinguisher service companies tracking NFPA 10 monthly, annual and 6-year schedules. InspectExtinguishers has already validated the angle of being priced out by enterprise tools (flat from $29 CAD, unlimited users), and Fire Inspect Hub lists $3.75/user ([Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1111275/inspectextinguishers); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10037388/Fire-Inspect-Hub/)). The twist is a US-focused, offline-first QR app with a "due next month" outreach list. Price at $39–79/month. Build takes 3–5 weeks. Indie entrants are arriving now.

**9. Chimney-sweep reports (59).** No dominant specialist turned up. Generic field-service tools offer chimney pages without NFPA 211 Level 1/2/3 report templates ([Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/industries/chimney-sweep-service-software/); [Kickserv](https://www.kickserv.com/industries/chimney-and-masonry)). Price at $29–49/month, build in 2–4 weeks. Demand peaks Sep–Dec, so outreach in late November is late for this season. Evidence is weak.

**10. Wholesale producer standing orders (54).** Buyers are bakeries and roasters with 20–80 café accounts that currently take standing orders by text and spreadsheet. Incumbents target distributors. Pepper, for example, raised $30M to serve large distributor catalogs ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/13/pepper-iconiq-startup-foodservice-ecommerce-30m)). Price at $49–99/month. There is plenty of room, but no demand evidence beyond inference, and it is a longer build.

### 11–12. The trading-card and Japan angles stay low

The hobby is booming. ICv2 puts US/Canada hobby games at **+40% to $3.66B in 2025** ([ICv2](https://icv2.com/articles/markets/view/62035/big-growth-hobby-games-2025)). But the B2B money goes to marketplaces and POS vendors. BinderPOS costs $100–150/month plus fees and is owned by TCGplayer/eBay, and ShadowPOS costs $200/month ([TCGplayer POS](https://seller.tcgplayer.com/point-of-sale); [ShadowPOS](https://shadowpos.com/)). Point tools are left with $15–50/month price points across a fragmented base of shops. **#11, a grading-submission tracker**, is easy to build in 1–2 weeks, and its one competitor, Grade Flow Tracker at $14.99, has no reviews ([Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/grading-submission-tracker)). But at $15–30/month, 1k MRR needs 35–65 shops. **#12, a Japanese-card pricing feed**, would use the founder's Japanese directly, but it fails on data access. Mercari JP bars commercial use of its data without written permission ([Mercari JP](https://gl.help.jp.mercari.com/en/guide/articles/gl_0053)). The US de minimis exemption ended on 2025-08-29 and a 15% tariff now applies to Japanese goods, which pushed collectors toward English cards ([CardCodex](https://cardcodex.com/blog/us-tariffs-influence-pokemon-card-hobby/)). Neither idea clears the "unusually strong evidence" bar. Before the penalty, #11 would score 63, so it is a reasonable weekend side bet, not the main path.

## What was cut, and why it matters

Several popular models fail the "money soon" test on this evidence. **Programmatic SEO with ads** needs 50k+ monthly pageviews for $1k at a $20 RPM. Google's March 2026 spam update targeted scaled content abuse, and template sites reportedly lost 30–50% of their visibility ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-march-2026-core-update-rolling-out-now-472759); [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/google-march-2026-core-update-impact-analysis-recovery)). **Shopify Scripts-replacement discount apps** have real demand, but the category is saturated: one thread names more than a dozen apps, including a free one and one at $15/month ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/shopify-scripts-are-ending-what-is-everyone-using-now/632089)).

**Slack Marketplace apps** have a real moat, because unlisted commercial apps are throttled to 1 request per minute on message history ([Slack changelog](https://docs.slack.dev/changelog/2025/05/29/rate-limit-changes-for-non-marketplace-apps)). But listing requires 10 active installs and a review lasting several weeks ([Slack changelog](https://docs.slack.dev/changelog/2026/09/01/slack-marketplace-install-requirement)), so revenue is 2–3 months out. They make a good second app. **Reddit lead-finders** are on 2026 saturation lists ([WeAreFounders](https://www.wearefounders.uk/10-saas-ideas-to-avoid-as-an-indie-hacker-in-2026/)), and Bosley's growth relied on aged accounts and purchased upvotes, which brings platform-ban risk. **QuickBooks data-import tools** and **self-storage lien workflows** were dropped because they edge into accounting and legal territory. **"Cheaper Jobber"** was dropped because dozens of alternatives already exist. **A Japanese sourcing desk** is service labor, and tariffs have worsened its economics.

## Top three, and a two-week test for the Stocky gap

**The recommendation:** lead with **#1, the Shopify receiving and cost ledger.** It has the only first-hand buyer pain in the research, a marketplace that charges nothing on the first $1M, buyers who identified themselves in public threads, and a wedge that complements competitors instead of copying them. Run **#2, the done-for-you trade text-back service**, as the fast-cash hedge. It can produce invoices in weeks while the app builds review history, but treat it as an experiment because its evidence rests on one post. Keep **#3, the vertical directory**, as the cheapest low-maintenance asset to build once #1 is live. Expect the first paid user in 3–6 weeks and $1k MRR in about 2–4 months if validation passes. $3k+ more likely takes 6–12 months.

**The plan below** ends with a go/no-go decision. Every outreach message is a draft for the founder to edit and send personally. Nothing goes out automatically.

| Days | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Audit competitors. Install or trial the top 5 apps for "receiving", "purchase orders" and "cost history" in a free Shopify development store. Record installs, ratings, last update, and whether each handles partial receipts, invoice reconciliation and dated cost. | Gap matrix. Kill the idea if two or more apps already do receiving and dated cost well and have solid reviews. |
| 2–3 | Build a list of about 60–100 prospects: participants in the Stocky threads ([1](https://community.shopify.com/t/stocky-is-ending-what-workflow-are-you-most-concerned-about-replacing/672546), [2](https://community.shopify.com/t/with-stocky-shutting-down-i-ve-been-trying-to-figure-out-what-to-use-instead/673369), [3](https://community.shopify.com/t/stocky-replacement/621332)), plus multi-location Shopify retailers found via store locators and Google Maps. | Spreadsheet with name, store, channel and the pain they stated |
| 3–4 | Make a one-page site with a 90-second Loom of a clickable prototype (receipt screen, then cost-history chart) and a "founding customer" offer: $29/mo locked for life, or $150 for 6 months prepaid. | Landing page and demo (~$12 domain, free hosting tiers) |
| 4–8 | Outreach in daily batches of 15–20. Run 20-minute calls asking: what did you pick after Stocky, what breaks at receiving, how do you track the cost you paid last quarter? | Target 10+ calls from about 100 touches, per the benchmark funnel |
| 6–10 | Concierge MVP: for the 2–3 most eager merchants, set up the audit-log archiver plus a cost snapshot feed (webhooks into a database, exported to Google Sheets). Build it by hand if needed. | Real usage. Ask for payment on day 7 of use. |
| 11–14 | Decide. **Go** if 3+ merchants pay or prepay, or 5+ of 10 rank receiving or cost history as their top unsolved pain. **Pivot to the audit-log exporter alone** if pain is real but narrow. **Stop** if fewer than 3 of 10 call their stopgap unacceptable. | Decision memo; if go, a 3–4 week build of the full ledger and an App Store submission |

A lot of merchants told me the Stocky replacements cover purchase orders but not receiving or historical cost, because Shopify only keeps the last cost you paid. I'm building a small add-on for exactly that. Could I ask you 3 questions on a 15-minute call this week? If it's useful, founding customers lock in $29/month. — [name]"

Two caveats apply to the plan. Shopify Community forum norms on promoting products were not checked, so read the rules before posting. The 3-paying-of-10 threshold is a judgment call based on the generic funnel benchmark, not a measured conversion rate for Shopify apps.

## Conclusion

The pattern across all five research streams is that money arrives fastest where an external force has just created a gap: a platform removing a feature, a regulator requiring a recurring form, or an incumbent priced for bigger customers. Clever technology matters less. Removal gaps like Stocky decay, though. The buyers in pain are the late, dissatisfied switchers, and the window is months, not years. That argues for validating #1 within two weeks rather than polishing it.

The evidence also shows that "first dollar in weeks" and "$1–10k MRR" are different problems. Service-shaped offers (#2) and outreach-sold listings (#3) solve the first. Marketplace apps and compliance-interval tools (#1, #5) solve the second, because customers stay as long as the workflow recurs. A founder who wants both should run one of each in parallel and drop whichever loses after the two-week test.
